Northern Lights Odds

Chance of seeing the aurora borealis at least once before Sep 2031, from the same stretch of eight past solar cycles, replayed against today's sky.

How this was made

Solar Cycle 25 peaked in Oct 2024, so the next five years are cycle years +2 to +7, the declining phase, when coronal-hole streams and late CMEs still throw big storms. I replayed those same years of cycles 17–24 (40 analog years) against the real 3-hourly Kp record (GFZ Potsdam, CC BY 4.0).

A cell counts as "aurora-capable" in a 3-hour slot if Kp was high enough that the auroral oval's equatorward edge (66.5° − 2.07·Kp geomagnetic latitude; Starkov 1994) sat within 3° of you (faint glow), on top of you (clear display) or 3° poleward of you (overhead), it was within 4 h of magnetic midnight, and the sun was below −12°. Geomagnetic latitude uses a centred dipole (pole 80.7°N, 72.7°W). Multi-year odds assume independent years: 1−(1−p)ⁿ.

Not included: cloud cover, light pollution, moonlight, and cycle-25's own strength. Storms at Kp ≥ 5 were of slots in the analog years, and Kp ≥ 7 just . 0% means none happened in the analogs, not impossible.

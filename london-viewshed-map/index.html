Method & sources

Each viewshed is a true line-of-sight calculation over a surface model of London: from an eye point (1.7 m above the ground on hills, at gallery height on towers), rays are cast in every direction across the 42 × 36 km study area, and a 20 m cell counts as visible only if the top of a 10 m structure standing in it (about three storeys) sits at least as high in the observer's field of view as everything between it and the observer. Earth curvature and standard atmospheric refraction (k = 0.13) are applied, which matters beyond about 10 km. Cells within 30–80 m of a viewpoint are cleared so a tower does not block its own view.

Buildings & trees mode uses the Environment Agency's LiDAR Composite Digital Surface Model (2 m, last return): every roof, treetop and gasometer that was there when the aircraft flew over. Each 20 m analysis cell keeps the median height of its hundred 2 m pixels: a cell counts as solid where more than half of it is, so a lone tree in a park does not become a wall (a 90th-percentile version blocked nearly everything from open hilltops, because trees are porous and rays pass between them). Bare earth mode uses the matching Digital Terrain Model, the ground with everything removed, tested against the same 10 m target. The gap between the two is what the built city takes away.

Caveats worth knowing: the 10 m target is a modelling choice. Asking whether bare ground is visible would show almost nothing from a hilltop over a roofscape, since at a grazing angle only the highest crest along each ray is ever seen; asking about a three-storey roofline is closer to what “can I see that part of London?” means. Change the target and every area moves, but the ranking of viewpoints and the built-versus-bare gap hold. The surface model includes tree canopy, so leafy hilltops read as more closed than the maintained sightlines their viewing indicators protect. Results are limited to the study area, so views running past its edges are clipped. “Visible” does not mean a person on the pavement there is in view. Hill viewpoints are snapped to the highest bare-earth cell within 200 m of the named spot, and tower heights are the operators' published gallery levels (Canary Wharf has no public gallery; its eye point is the top floor).

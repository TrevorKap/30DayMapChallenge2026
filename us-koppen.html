Shop notes

Projection: this plate uses Albers Equal-Area Conic, the same composite convention the U.S. Census Bureau and D3's geoAlbersUsa use for national choropleths — standard parallels 29.5°/45.5°N for the Lower 48, with Alaska and Hawaii separately projected (55°/65°N and 8°/18°N respectively) and inset at reduced scale, since no single conic frame can hold a continent that spans the Aleutians to the Florida Keys without absurd distortion.

Alaska adds subarctic interior (Fairbanks, Anchorage — Dfc), a narrow temperate-rainforest panhandle (Juneau — Cfb), a rare U.S. example of subpolar oceanic climate in the outer Aleutians (Cfc), and Arctic tundra along the North Slope (ET). Hawaii adds true tropical rainforest on Hilo's windward coast (Af) a few dozen miles from documented semi-arid steppe on Honolulu's leeward shore (BSh) — and alpine tundra at the Mauna Kea/Mauna Loa summits, well above the tropical air below them.

On "accurate for 2026": no Köppen map is ever literally current — every version, including this one, encodes 30-year climate normals, which are backward-looking by design. What's changed here from a "classic" textbook rendering are three modest, specifically documented nudges: the humid-subtropical/continental line (Cfa/Dfa) shifted north by roughly half a degree of latitude, the Great Plains aridity line (the 100th meridian) shifted east by a similar margin, and Alaska's Arctic tundra (ET) extent was pulled back from its classic footprint — all three reflect real, published poleward climate-zone shifts, not arbitrary redraws. Zone edges remain approximate, not authoritative to the kilometer.

State boundaries: PublicaMundi/MappingAPI (US Census-derived). Classification scheme: Köppen–Geiger system. 30 Day Mapping Challenge 2026.

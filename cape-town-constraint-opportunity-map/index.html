Cape Town · Constraint & Opportunity

What Cape Town can't build on, and where it could

The mountain, the coast and the wetlands hem the city in: about – of the metro's land carries a serious constraint. Against that, this map looks for undeveloped ground inside the urban fabric that is close to trains, MyCiTi and jobs. Only about – of the land qualifies, and a slice of it is contested, good ground that also sits on a floodplain, a dune belt or the airport's noise cone. Hover or tap to see what drives any spot.

Method & sources

Each cell in the City of Cape Town's mainland gets six constraint factors and three opportunity factors, each scaled 0 to 1. Constraint combines them so that any one severe limit binds and several accumulate: 1 − ∏(1 − capᵢ·factorᵢ). The factors and caps are protected land (nature reserves and national park from OpenStreetMap, cap 1.0; umbrella designations such as the Cape Floral Region heritage site and the biosphere reserve are left out, as are marine zones), steep slope (10% grade scores 0, 25% and steeper scores 1, cap 0.9), flood and water table (proximity to rivers, wetlands and open water, falling to 0 at 250 m, plus a damped local-depression term, cap 0.8), coastal exposure (land below 5 m within 3.5 km of the sea, plus a 150 m shoreline setback, cap 0.7), fire interface (share of tree and shrub cover within about 400 m, counted only near built-up land, cap 0.55) and airport noise (distance to the runways, full within 400 m and gone by 2.4 km, cap 0.6).

Opportunity is land availability times location. Availability comes from ESA WorldCover 10 m classes (bare 0.95, grass 0.8, shrub 0.55, cropland 0.5, trees 0.2, built-up 0), applied only inside the urban matrix (built-up share within about 600 m ramping from 10% to 40%) and zeroed where OpenStreetMap shows land that is spoken for: parks, pitches, golf courses, schools, hospitals, cemeteries, places of worship, military land, airfield, rail land, reservoirs, landfill and quarries. Mapped brownfield and construction sites count at 0.9. Location is 55% transit (rail stations within 1.2 km, MyCiTi stops within 600 m at 0.8 weight) and 45% jobs (density of commercial, retail and industrial land within about 1.4 km). Scores are stretched to the 96th percentile, cells at 0.28 or higher count as opportunity, and only blobs at least about 3 ha and 150 m across are kept, which drops rail and road verges. A cell is constrained above 0.40 and contested when it is both.

Limits: this is a screening exercise, not planning advice. It has no cadastre, ownership, zoning, bulk infrastructure or heritage overlay, so "open land" may be private, planned or already allocated. Informal settlements read as built-up, so densification there is not captured, and cropland such as the Philippi Horticultural Area is partly scored as available even though it is contested for good reasons. Distances are straight-line, not walking routes. MyCiTi stops come from OpenStreetMap route relations and rail stations from OpenStreetMap, and neither checks whether a line is running today. Elevation is a roughly 30 m global model with vertical noise, so slope and low-spot terms are coarse. The fire and airport terms are proxies, not hazard or noise contours. The weights and caps are judgment calls, not fitted to observed flooding, fires or development outcomes. The landmark table is a sanity check: the build script fails if the mountain does not outrank the CBD or the airport does not carry the strongest airport-noise score.

Where Paris clusters its culture, its nights and its books

Every museum, gallery, theatre and cinema; every bar, pub and club; every bookshop and antiques dealer in OpenStreetMap, sorted by density-based clustering (DBSCAN). A place only counts as a hot spot if venues sit within a couple of minutes' walk of each other, so isolated addresses drop out. Toggle an interest, hover a hot spot, or pick one from the ranking.

Method & sources

Follows Milan Janosov's "Identifying topical hot spots in urban areas": collect OpenStreetMap points of interest for a few categories, then let scikit-learn's DBSCAN find the dense pockets. His post is paywalled, so the categories and parameters here are my own choices in the same recipe. Culture = museums, galleries, theatres, cinemas, arts centres; Nightlife = bars, pubs, nightclubs; Books = bookshops and antiques shops. All were pulled from the Overpass API inside the Paris commune boundary (nodes plus building/area centroids, de-duplicated by position and name).

DBSCAN ran on haversine distance with ε = 130 m for every interest and a minimum of 8 (culture), 12 (nightlife) or 6 (books) venues within ε for a venue to seed a cluster. I picked these from a grid sweep: above ~150 m the chain-linking behaviour of DBSCAN fuses the whole Right Bank into one blob, and much below 100 m only a handful of clusters survive. The number of hot spots is an output, not an input. Each hull is the convex hull of the cluster's venues, padded by 65 m, so long thin streets such as a single bar row still get a visible area; convex hulls overstate the footprint of crescent-shaped clusters. Hot-spot names are the nearest OSM quarter / suburb / neighbourhood label to the cluster centre, so two hot spots can share a name.

OSM coverage is uneven: bars and shops that are tagged poorly or not at all won't appear, and a venue counted once per OSM object is not a measure of its size or footfall. Sources: OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0 (via Overpass API) · basemap and rendering by Mapbox GL JS. Data pulled 2026-09-19.

US–Mexico Border · 24-Hour Flow Model

The Crossing Hours

Nineteen real US–Mexico land crossings, each its own official CBP port of entry in federal crossing data — including the two metros where CBP tracks bridges separately, El Paso vs. Ysleta and Calexico vs. Calexico East. Marker size and color reflect each port's real 2024 crossing volume1: teal ports run heavier on personal vehicles and pedestrians, orange ports run heavier on commercial trucking. Every dot stands for roughly 400 modeled crossings and follows the real highway network between two interior cities; blue dots are northbound, entering the US, green dots are southbound, entering Mexico. Watch the tide turn with the morning and evening rush, and the smaller ports go quiet overnight. Click a port to fly to its real road network.

Ports, by real 2024 volume

1 Every port here is a real CBP land border crossing with its own record in the Bureau of Transportation Statistics' Border Crossing/Entry Data, queried directly from BTS's public dataset for calendar-year 2024: Personal Vehicle Passengers + Pedestrians (each port's "commuter" volume) and Trucks (its "commercial" volume). Where CBP itself reports a metro's bridges as separate ports — El Paso vs. Ysleta, and Calexico vs. Calexico East — they're kept separate here rather than merged into one node. A port is classified commercial-dominant when trucks make up at least 3.5% of its 2024 total; that includes Laredo, whose 7.7 million trucks in 2024 alone make it the single busiest commercial crossing on the border. Ports under roughly 15 million total 2024 crossings are modeled with reduced overnight hours; larger ports run around the clock in the model. Marker size scales with each port's real total 2024 volume. The road each dot follows is a real driving route from the Mapbox Directions API between the interior city on each side (e.g. Laredo↔San Antonio, Laredo↔Monterrey) — it follows the actual highway network, not a straight line; where a route request fails, that one port falls back to a straight line between the same two real endpoints. Hour-by-hour flow shapes, the north/south commute skew, and the fixed "1 dot ≈ 400 crossings" value remain a modeled simulation — CBP doesn't publish hour-level crossing counts — but every port's relative size, category, and total volume are real, sourced 2024 figures, not estimates.

Sources: Bureau of Transportation Statistics, Border Crossing/Entry Data (Socrata dataset, queried directly for this piece) · BTS, Border Crossing Data Annual Release 2023–2024

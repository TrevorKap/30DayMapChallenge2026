Method & sources

Built from Milan Janosov's "How Walkable Is Your City? Analyzing Urban Access with Python & OSM" (#30DayMapChallenge), which routes across real OpenStreetMap street graphs instead of drawing radius circles. Here every walkable way in the urban core of Miami, Miami Beach and Coral Gables (25.70–25.86°N, 80.305–80.12°W) was pulled from the Overpass API and, minus motorways, trunk roads, steps and no-foot-access links, built into a weighted NetworkX graph (largest connected component; 101,166 intersections, 154,821 street segments, edge weights = haversine meters). Mid-block shape vertices were merged away, so curved streets draw as straight chords between intersections.

The graph ships with the page and Dijkstra runs in your browser on every click: shortest network distance from the snapped origin to every intersection, divided by pace (walk 80 m/min, bike 250 m/min). A segment takes the color of the slower of its two ends, in 5-minute bands out to 30. Not modeled: traffic lights, drawbridge openings, elevation (Miami is flat), and the bike mode ignores one-way rules, bike-lane quality and safety — it is the same graph at a faster pace, so treat it as an upper bound. Streets beyond the study box are cut off at its edge.

Sources: OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0 (street network, via Overpass API) · basemap and rendering by Mapbox GL JS. Data pulled 2026-09-19.

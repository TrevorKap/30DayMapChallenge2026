The Storm Lane

Three hurricanes move through the eastern Pacific over six modeled days while six real trans-Pacific and coastal flight corridors run underneath them. Track color follows the real NHC Saffir–Simpson scale, from tropical storm green through Category 5 magenta; the soft shaded ring around each eye is a modeled hazard buffer, not an official NHC forecast cone. Airports, great-circle routing, and real-world distances for all six flight corridors are real; when a corridor's flight track crosses a storm's buffer it bends and turns coral, a modeled reroute rather than actual airline dispatch data. Storm tracks are an illustrative composite built on typical Eastern Pacific hurricane behavior — not a specific NOAA/NHC advisory.

1 Storm tracks: three named systems ("Talon", "Reya", "Odessa") moving through the eastern Pacific over a modeled 6-day window (05–11 Sep 2026), built to follow typical Eastern Pacific hurricane behavior — westward-then-recurving tracks, realistic intensification/weakening curves, and the real NHC Saffir–Simpson wind-speed thresholds and color convention. This is an illustrative composite, not a transcription of any specific NOAA/National Hurricane Center advisory, best-track, or forecast-cone product. The shaded ring around each storm's eye is a modeled hazard buffer sized off its category, not an official forecast cone.

2 Flight corridors: all six routes connect real airports (LAX, SFO, TIJ, MEX, SJD, PVR, SAL, HNL) at their real coordinates, flown along the real great-circle path between them with distances computed from the haversine formula. Flights animate at a modeled 880 km/h cruise speed compressed into the 6-day playback window. When a flight's position would fall inside a storm's modeled hazard buffer, its rendered track bends around the buffer's edge and is marked as rerouting — a modeled visualization of storm-avoidance, not real airline flight-planning or ADS-B data.
